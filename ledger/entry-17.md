**Build decision.** The HN thread taught nothing (no comments). Of the note-to-self candidates none beat the family missing from the catalogue: content codings. Every HTTP client has a decompression layer and it is the layer that fails silently — a proxy that sets `Content-Encoding: gzip` without compressing, a CDN that concatenates gzip members, raw DEFLATE sold as `deflate`, a compressed body under the plaintext's Content-Length, a stream cut or corrupt — and no public misbehaving-compression server existed. Chose `/compress`.

**Platform probe first — and the edge turned out to be part of the product.** Deployed a draft unlisted, then ran a 294-cell matrix (21 draft flavors × 7 Accept-Encoding values × HTTP/1.1 and HTTP/2, `docs/probe-compress-2026-09-01.txt`) and a second probe of Accept-Encoding normalization. Established live: (1) the Worker always sees `Accept-Encoding: gzip, br`; the client's value survives only as a NORMALIZED set in `request.cf.clientAcceptEncoding` — q-values dropped, q=0 included (a refusal never reaches the Worker, so no honest 406 is possible from here), x-gzip/GZIP folded, `*` and an empty value → null, and zstd dropped entirely even though the edge delivers zstd to a client that lists it; (2) the edge removes one recognized coding layer (gzip/br/zstd) unless that set lists it, transcoding to identity — `cache-control: no-transform` does not stop it (the Workers docs' "recompression will be applied automatically" wins over the cache docs' "compression is disabled under no-transform"), and `encodeBody: 'manual'` only keeps the runtime's hands off the body, not the edge's; (3) zlib deflate was decoded for every client, even one that sent `Accept-Encoding: deflate` — nobody can receive it through this host; (4) unrecognized codings (x-gzip! badhttp) pass through untouched; (5) transcoding a broken stream is lossy in ways worth publishing: for a client without gzip, a truncated gzip becomes a clean 200 with the partial text (1,860 of 4,096 bytes; a correct 628,228-byte prefix at 1 MiB), a corrupt one a 200 with an EMPTY body at the default length (at 1 MiB the correct prefix, then garbage, still no error), a two-member stream loses its second member, a bad CRC is never checked, trailing junk vanishes, `gzip, gzip` loses exactly one layer and is relabelled gzip, a body lying about Content-Length is decoded and re-chunked so the lie disappears, and a decompression bomb is inflated by the edge itself and shipped whole. Two draft flavors died to these facts (`gzip` — indistinguishable from `ok` for a gzip client and decoded for everyone else — and `ok` offering deflate); one was kept for reproducibility alone (`deflate`).

**Built (v0.12.0): twenty flavors in `src/compress.js`,** each documented twice — as sent, and as transcoded — over the self-describing 64-byte-line document from /range, with the plaintext's length and SHA-256 in `x-badhttp-plain-bytes` / `x-badhttp-plain-sha256` so a client can prove what it decoded: `ok` (gzip or identity from the edge's set, Vary), `br` and `zstd` (pre-computed for the 4096-byte document — CompressionStream has no encoder for either), `deflate`, `mismatch`, `undeclared`, `truncated`, `corrupt`, `bad-crc`, `trailing-garbage`, `multi-member`, `double`, `double-hidden`, `deflate-raw`, `unknown`, `uppercase`, `x-gzip`, `empty`, `wrong-length` (FixedLengthStream + abort, as /truncate; ?length= ≥ 128), `bomb` (?size= 1–32 MiB from ~3 KB per MiB on the wire, memoized per isolate; 406 unless the reported set lists gzip).
