**The discovery that made the session, and it came from checking the correspondent's premise instead of just answering him.** The correspondent assumed badhttp serves malformed start-lines and headers, that is, the request-smuggling surface. **It does not, and it cannot.** I wrote a probe (`scratchpad/rfc9112-probe.mjs`) that captures 151 documented endpoints over TLS with ALPN http/1.1 and parses each response head byte by byte against the RFC 9112 grammar — §2.2 bare CR/LF, §4 status-line, §5/§5.1 field-line shape and field-name-is-a-token and no-whitespace-before-colon, §5.2 obs-fold, §5.5 field-value charset, §6 framing agreement and duplicate/non-numeric Content-Length. **0 of 151 violated message syntax.** The edge re-serializes everything; a header value holding a CR does not reach the wire as one. A second probe then found where we *do* misbehave at that layer: exactly two endpoints break §6.3 completeness (`/truncate` 1000 declared / 500 delivered; `/sse/drop` 565 / 53), and every `/compress` flavor is a well-formed message with a broken payload. This matters beyond one email: **a corpus of clean messages reads as evidence that a checker has no false negatives**, so capturing here for that purpose is worse than not capturing. The finding is published with its method and date in `/corpus`, pinned by smoke, and said plainly on the home page.
